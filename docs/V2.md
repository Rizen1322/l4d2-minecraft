# V2: transport, rendering and mob behavior

Both co-op PCs should update with both games closed, then restart their guests
and bridges. The LAN/VPN setup and normal Minecraft launcher remain unchanged.

## Movement and delivery

Avatar and mob poses now travel as latest snapshots, separately from reliable
edits/attacks. Building queues cannot hold up every newer Steve pose. Only
unnumbered events are reordered for combat priority; numbered events retain
cumulative-ACK order. A same-session authenticated reconnect replaces its old
socket, including when the room is full. A different session cannot take over
an occupied survivor.

Freshness follows the original guest sample time, not retransmission time.
Disconnected mobs no longer linger as apparently fresh targets. Older hosts can
still negotiate the previous reliable transport.

Remote Steve/entity positions interpolate for rendering. Duplicate samples do
not restart the blend; genuine teleports snap. This does not change server-side
hit validation, native Source collision or the local camera. Interpolation reduces
visible jumps; it is not client prediction and cannot remove latency.

## World composition and teammate glow

Minecraft captures a wider world view with a 20% tangent guard band. Exported
FOV describes that actual capture, and Steve sampling uses the same projection.
No window-size changes are needed. Extra pixels help during rapid turns but
cannot reconstruct surfaces hidden in the captured frame. Thin geometry and
approximate Source depth can still cause clipping.

Bridged survivors transfer native glow to a six-box Steve silhouette made by
this project. One non-solid proxy is created per survivor. Native survivor glow
is disabled during ownership and restored afterward. Explicit glow color is
preserved; default cyan/red reflects health. The shape is an upright outline,
not the guest's animated/armored mesh. Local pin poses still render in Minecraft.

## Combat and mobs

Confirmed shield contacts play Minecraft's block sound. Exhausting shield capacity
plays its break sound and starts the existing cooldown; normal item durability
remains separate. Sound receipts are deduplicated and lower priority than critical
Source replies. Sword/golem hits use native slash/club damage with impact position
and force. Source controls blood/death/ragdoll behavior; hits do not spawn extra
particle entities. Existing diamond/golem common-infected one-hit rules remain.

Ground routes survive refreshes. Replies resume near current position instead of
sending mobs back to an old request origin. Moving away to reach stairs counts
as progress, and a pending first route does not trigger the short stuck-target
timer. Low ledges use guest jumping plus Source terrain checks.

The registry is no longer restricted to golems. Owned ground mobs can wander;
hostiles use survivor proxies, golems use infected proxies, and brain-driven
hostiles also receive attack-target memories. Dead/stale targets are rejected;
Source validates impact line of sight again. Common infected, specials and Tanks
can pursue registered Minecraft mobs. Pinned specials retain their existing victim.

**Generic integration does not implement every vanilla ability.** Flying routes,
water navigation, boss phases and special-infected pin animations against guest
mobs need separate implementations. Infected-to-mob contact damage uses proximity
and line of sight. Neutral mobs keep vanilla targeting rules. Ground AI depends
on the native navmesh and bounded surface traces. The nearest 64 guest mobs are
reported; at most 16 non-solid native targets are registered per survivor context.
Large spawn crowds can exceed those limits.

## Verification

- 137 Python tests, including real loopback WebSocket congestion, replacement
  reconnects, stale-entity expiration and reliable remote combat delivery.
- Fabric/Java policy and motion/projection tests; native x86/cursor checks.
- 11 Squirrel suites, including shield sounds, glow ownership/restoration,
  native hit force, corpse rejection and Tank priority over horde enumeration.
- WPF build/tests, including RU/EN and child-process arguments.
- Retail L4D2 API probe: glow proxy creation, original glow disabling/restoration,
  `TakeDamageEx` support and `CommandABot` acceptance. ReShade compiled the shader.

A fresh two-PC flight/combat run and visual/audio confirmation remain necessary.
These checks do not establish complete correctness on every map or a measured
reduction in network latency.
